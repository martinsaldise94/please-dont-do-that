# Rules v1 — Catalog

Rules v1 use AST heuristics on parsed HTML/JSX. Each rule answers one question:
**Could this element belong to another business unchanged?** If yes, it fires.

Rules are grouped into four dimensions. Dimension weights in the AI Smell score:
Copy 40% · Layout 30% · Visual 20% · Credibility 10%.

---

## Copy dimension

### C01 — Generic buzzwords

**Could it belong to another business?** Words like *innovation*, *excellence*, and *seamless* describe nothing specific. They can be dropped into any landing page without editing.

**Fires when:** ≥2 buzzwords found in headings or body text. Language-aware: uses the EN or ES dictionary based on detected content language.

**Severity:** `low` (2–3 hits) · `medium` (4–6) · `high` (7+)

**EN dictionary sample:** innovation, excellence, seamless, leverage, empower, streamline, game-changing, world-class, holistic, scalable, disruptive…

**ES dictionary sample:** innovación, excelencia, soluciones, vanguardia, potenciar, transformador, escalable, disruptivo, calidad total…

---

### C02 — Generic CTAs

**Could it belong to another business?** "Get Started", "Book Now", "Try for Free" are the same buttons on a million sites. They carry zero business identity.

**Fires when:** any CTA button or link text matches a known generic phrase (case-insensitive, EN or ES).

**Severity:** always `medium`

**EN phrases:** get started, try for free, start free trial, book a demo, join today, sign up free, get a quote, reserve a table, book now…

**ES phrases:** comenzar ahora, prueba gratis, empieza ya, reserva una demo, solicitar cita, reservar mesa, ver menú, apúntate ahora…

---

### C03 — Trust language

**Could it belong to another business?** Phrases like "trusted by thousands" or "join millions of users" are stock credibility claims with no verifiable backing. They appear verbatim across unrelated industries.

**Fires when:** body text matches a trust-claim pattern (regex, EN or ES).

**Severity:** always `medium`

**EN patterns:** *trusted by thousands*, *loved by customers*, *join thousands of*, *over N+ clients/users*, *N+ happy clients*…

**ES patterns:** *confían en nosotros*, *miles de clientes*, *más de N clientes*, *únete a miles*…

## Layout dimension

### L01 — Classic SaaS template

**Could it belong to another business?** The hero → features → testimonials → pricing sequence is the canonical AI-generated landing page structure. Any company can be dropped into this skeleton.

**Fires when:** ≥2 of the following sections are detected: `hero`, `features`, `testimonials`, `pricing`, `cta`.

**Severity:** `medium` (2 sections) · `high` (3+ sections)

**Section detection:** class/id names, semantic tags (`<header>`, `<footer>`), heading text patterns (*Why choose*, *What we offer*, *What our clients say*…).

---

### L02 — Repeated feature cards

**Could it belong to another business?** A grid of 3–4 cards with icon + title + description is the default way AI represents "features" or "benefits". The structure is identical regardless of industry.

**Fires when:** ≥3 elements matching card/feature/benefit class patterns are found.

**Severity:** `medium`

---

### L03 — Minimum viable landing page

**Could it belong to another business?** Even the simplest generic site has a hero section, a descriptive heading, and a CTA button. This combo alone is a weak signal of genericness.

**Fires when:** a hero section is present AND ≥1 heading AND ≥1 CTA text is detected.

**Severity:** always `low`

---

## Visual dimension

### V01 — Gradient overload

**Could it belong to another business?** Multi-stop gradients — especially purple-to-indigo or blue-to-cyan — are the signature aesthetic of AI-generated SaaS sites. They carry no brand identity.

**Fires when:** total gradient count ≥2, counting Tailwind `from-*`/`to-*`/`via-*`/`bg-gradient-*` classes plus CSS `linear-gradient` occurrences in `<style>` blocks.

**Severity:** `low` (2–4) · `medium` (5–7) · `high` (8+)

---

### V02 — Blur blobs

**Could it belong to another business?** Large blurred circles or ovals used as background decoration are a visual cliché of AI-generated design. They appear identically on unrelated sites.

**Fires when:** ≥2 Tailwind blur utility classes (`blur-xl`, `blur-2xl`, `blur-3xl`) or ≥2 CSS `filter: blur` / `backdrop-filter` declarations.

**Severity:** `medium`

---

### V03 — Generic SaaS palette

**Could it belong to another business?** Purple, indigo, and blue dominate AI-generated SaaS sites. The exact same palette appears on productivity tools, analytics platforms, and B2B software regardless of what they actually do.

**Fires when:** ≥4 Tailwind color classes in the purple/violet/indigo/blue/sky/cyan family.

**Severity:** `low` (4–7 classes) · `medium` (8+)

---

## Credibility dimension

### CR01 — Generic testimonials

**Could it belong to another business?** AI-generated testimonials have a recognizable pattern: star rating, generic praise, vague first name or initials. Real customer quotes are idiosyncratic and specific.

**Fires when:** ≥1 testimonial element is found (blockquote, class `testimonial`/`review`/`quote`/`testimonio`/`opinion`).

**Severity:** `medium` (1 testimonial) · `high` (≥2 testimonials AND ≥2 star-rating patterns)

---

### CR02 — Sourceless statistics

**Could it belong to another business?** "98% satisfaction rate", "500+ clients", "$2M saved" are common AI filler stats. Real businesses cite specific, verifiable numbers tied to their actual product.

**Fires when:** ≥2 of the following are found in body text: percentages (`\d+%`), social-proof counts (`N+ clients/users/customers/businesses`), large revenue claims (`£2M`, `$1M+`).

**Severity:** `medium` (2–3 patterns) · `high` (4+)
