Our Story

From Évora to Regent Street

Ana Ferreira grew up in a farmhouse outside Évora, in the low cork-oak plains of the Alentejo. Her grandmother, Avó Lurdes, kept a wood-fired oven going six days a week. Ana moved to London at 22, trained at Leith's, then spent three years cooking under José Pizarro in Bermondsey before moving to Bristol with her partner Tom in 2018.

They opened Oleander's in April 2019 with 28 covers, a handwritten menu board, and a wine list of nothing but Portuguese labels. The Regent Street site — a former florist's, which explains the name — seats 36 now and has a small heated terrace for five months of the year.

Everything on the menu is cooked by Ana and her sous chef, Marcus Webb, a Bristol local who came via Wilsons and The Cauldron. The front of house is run by Tom Ferreira, who has an alarming enthusiasm for Alentejano wines and will talk you through them at length if you let him.

Chef-Owner

"I cook the food I'd want to eat after a long day. Nothing fussy. The bread comes from Hart's Bakery on Elder Road, the lamb is from Pipers Farm in Devon, and the salt cod I still buy from a supplier my mother has used in Évora for thirty years. That traceability matters to me."

The Wine List

Tom has put together a list of around 50 Portuguese wines — almost nothing else. There are everyday bottles from the Alentejo and Dão starting at £28, and a small back-list of older Bairrada and Colares if you want to push the boat out. By-the-glass selection rotates and is always at least 8 options.
